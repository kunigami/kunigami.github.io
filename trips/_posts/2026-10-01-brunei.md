## Bandar Seri Begawan

The city name means *City of the Blessed One* in Malay. Seri Begawan was Sanskrit origin, which on itself is a nice link to the [Brunei](https://www.kuniga.me/docs/history/brunei.html)'s history, in particular the early Indian influence.

The first thing one notices about Brunei is that it's very car centric, like the US. Similarly the roads are filled with SUVs and few buses. There is a rideshare app called Dart that only works in Brunei, and it's only used for requesting a car - payment still needs to be in cash.

I've only seen a small part of the country, but the parts we saw were pretty clean and the public spaces well maintained.

The weather was hot and humid, so we often sought relief in air conditioned shopping malls and museums. We visited a few such as the Royal Regalia Museum, which has some of the gifts the Sultan of Brunei received from other countries. I found the collection interesting. One that caught my eye was this painting of a temple we saw from a trip to Vietnam, the One Pillar Pagoda (Chùa Một Cột).

We also visited the museum Balai Khazanah Islam Sultan Haji Hassanal Bolkiah which is dedicated to the Islamic history. It has nice miniatures of the 2 major mosques that we visited. It also has some section on science, which displayed a curta calculator! Which I saw before in the [Computer History Museum](https://www.kuniga.me/blog/2025/02/07/computer-history-museum.html).

Another connection from a previous trip: I learned the madrassa of [Al-Qarawiyyin](https://www.kuniga.me/trips/2025/11/08/morocco.html) in Fez, Morocco, was founded as a mosque by a woman, Fatima al-Fihri. Other than these, I didn't find the museum super interesting. 

We did a boat tour around the Kampong Ayer. One of my favorite things was spotting a proboscis monkey up close. We later went to Sabah for river cruises and saw many monkeys but none this close.

One of the most fascinating things I learned is that the word compound as in "a set of buildings" comes from *Kampong*, meaning village in Malay, whereas compound as in "something made of several parts" comes from Latin. Same spelling with different origins! It's possible that Kampung made into Malay from European sources, in particular the Portuguese via Malacca (see [Malacca History](https://www.kuniga.me/docs/history/malaysia.html#portuguese-malacca-1511-1641)).
