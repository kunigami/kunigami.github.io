I'm using the term *atomic post* in reference to the concept of [atomic habits]({{books}}/2025/10/18/atomic-habits.html). The idea is for posts to be as narrow as possible in scope, while still being self-contained.

<!--more-->

Like with atomic habits, the idea is to lower the bar for posting in hopes of posting more often. I mentioned before the desire to write [shower thoughts]({{blog}}/2024/12/02/centralizing-thoughts.html) more often and that:

> I still need a forcing function to make such posts, because otherwise I keep waiting until I have enough to write about them and it never happens.

In software development we strive for functions to be **modular**: short but also conceptually self-contained and with the right level of abstraction, so that they can be more easily reused. For posts, the same idea applies, but the 'reuse' is so that I can reference the idea in other posts and avoid repeating the same argument over and over.

A new motivation for doing this is that I started using LLMs to find connections between posts and it sometimes surprises me. My expectation is that the more content I have out the higher the chances of interesting connections.

I've set up some methodology to help with this. I keep Google docs for several topics I want to write about and whenever I read or think of something related, I add it to that document (this very post had a corresponding doc!). Whenever I find I have enough ideas collected, I will post them.

I'm also trying to be more deliberate on what [non-ficcion books]({{books}}/) I read: I'll pick topics I already plan to write about instead of random ones that happen to seem interesting to me at the time. The first example of this is [Elbow Room]({{books}}/2026/05/19/elbow-room.html) by Daniel C. Dennett, since I'm interested in sharing my ideas on free will.

Once I have the notes, it should take me, ideally, less than an hour to finish the post. This way I don't have to find a large block of time to write it. I won't worry about being comprehensive, because I can always go back and edit the post.

## Related Posts

In [On Memory]({{blog}}/2022/12/27/on-memory.html) I described keeping book notes in Google Docs, but I'm gradually shifting to adding the notes under the a topic I'm interested in instead. Only when it's something I hadn't thought about or thoughts on the book/writing itself do I keep under the book note.

As early as 2021, in [Writing Posts]({{blog}}/2021/09/01/writing-posts.html) I mention I keep post drafts in Google Docs, but I do that when I already started the process of writing the post, while the new process is at an earlier stage, during ideation.
